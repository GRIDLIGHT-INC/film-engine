Set description 3855 chars · 6 sections · prompt source
Room layout geography · 780 chars −

One long rectangular room. At the NORTH end, the big front window onto the harbour, with the entrance door beside it. Running the full length of the WEST side, the chrome-edged counter with its row of bolted swivel stools, the backbar behind it, and the pass through to the kitchen. Running the full length of the EAST side, the four red booths against the wall, each under a window. At the SOUTH end, opposite the harbour window, the back wall: the brass cash register on a stand, the coat hooks, the door to the restrooms, and a mirrored panel that reflects the length of the room back toward the water. The centre aisle of checkerboard tile runs north to south between counter and booths. The corner booth in use is the last one, at the north end nearest the window.

The place tone · 300 chars −

AN AMERICAN HARBOURSIDE DINER, 1955. Built 1941, re-topped and re-upholstered in 1952, kept immaculate ever since — the owner polishes it daily. It gleams. Postwar optimism in chrome, and the wrong room to have the worst conversation of your life in.

Architecture surfaces · 640 chars −

Streamline-moderne prefab shell. Chrome-edged counter, chrome-pedestal swivel stools with saturated red vinyl seats, unsplit and bright. Stainless backsplash polished to a mirror. Mirrored strip along the backbar doubling the room. Deep red vinyl banquettes with tight piping. Boomerang-pattern formica tabletops in cream and red with bright chrome edge banding, newer than the building. Black-and-white checkerboard floor tile, waxed, a faint traffic path worn pale down the centre aisle. Pressed-tin ceiling, slow ceiling fans, milk-glass pendant globes, lit.

The north window hard constraint −

A big sheet of CLEAR plate glass — clean and transparent, with nothing painted on it except the name. Painted across the glass in a period hand-lettered script are the words THE GLASS HARBOUR DINER, GLASS beginning with the letter G and HARBOUR beginning with the letter H; from inside the room this lettering is mirrored and reads backwards. A neon OPEN sign, also reversed from in here.

Outside that window background plate −

A real, actual working harbour, photographed through the clear glass — a genuine location in real depth and real perspective. Moored fishing boats with masts, rigging and hulls on dark water. A timber jetty with bollards and coiled rope. A stone breakwater. A lighthouse out on the point. Gulls. Parked 1950s cars at the kerb. The low sun sits over the water throwing a broken glitter path across it.
